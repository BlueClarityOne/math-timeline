Riemann hypothesisVerified proofHow far can the count of prime numbers stray from the pattern we expect?Impact3/5Mathematicians can now use a proven bound on how far prime counts stray from their predicted pattern.

What we know

Levent Alpöge worked with Claude to prove the prediction. Independent teams checked both the argument and the exact question it answers. Clay’s prize review is separate.

Why it matters

Mathematicians can use that limit on how unevenly primes are spread. Many results that assumed the limit now have a checked foundation.

Still open

P vs NPOpenIf a computer can quickly check an answer, can it quickly find one?Potential impact5/5If P = NP yields a fast enough algorithm, a forged payment signature could pass the same checks as a genuine one.

What we know

Nobody has proved whether every problem with a quickly checkable answer can also be solved efficiently. Here, efficient means the work grows at most as a polynomial in the input size.

Why it matters

The 5/5 scenario requires a practical algorithm showing P = NP. It could undermine digital signatures and make complex scheduling far easier. A proof that P ≠ NP would settle a fundamental limit on computing and leave that scenario unrealised.

Solved without AI

Poincaré conjectureHuman proofIf every loop in a closed 3D space can shrink to a point, must that space be a 3D sphere?Impact3/5Perelman gave mathematicians a way to identify a closed three-dimensional space as a sphere from the behaviour of its loops.

What we know

Grigori Perelman published the proof in 2002–03. Mathematicians checked it over several years. Clay awarded the Millennium Prize in 2010.

Why it matters

His work settled how to recognise the three-dimensional sphere and helped classify the possible shapes of three-dimensional spaces.

What we could lose

A payment network checks a signature before it moves money. If someone could efficiently produce a valid signature for your account, the network could accept a transfer you never approved. A practical algorithm for P = NP could make that possible.

What we could gain

The same advance could help hospitals find rosters that cover every shift, meet rest rules and put the right specialists on call. A computer can already check whether a proposed roster works.
