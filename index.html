Settled with AI

Riemann hypothesisHow far can the count of prime numbers stray from the pattern we expect?Impact 3/5 · Several research areasVerified proof

What we know

Levent Alpöge worked with Claude to prove the prediction. Independent teams checked both the argument and the exact question it answers. Clay’s prize review is separate.

Why it matters

Mathematicians can use that limit on how unevenly primes are spread. Many results that assumed the limit now have a checked foundation.

Erdős unit distanceCan an arrangement of dots beat Erdős’s predicted limit on pairs exactly one unit apart?Impact 1/5 · One questionDisproved

What we know

Yes. An OpenAI model found a family of arrangements with more such pairs than the conjecture allowed. Nine mathematicians checked the counterexample.

Why it matters

A prediction from 1946 failed. Geometers now have a higher minimum for what is achievable. The maximum is still unknown.

See the evidence
  • Noga Alon and co-authors · Independent companion paper
  • OpenAI · Construction and announcement
Cycle double coverCan loops cover every link in a network exactly twice?Impact 2/5 · One research areaProved

What we know

An OpenAI model proved this works whenever removing a single link cannot disconnect the network. Graph theorists published independent accounts of the proof.

Why it matters

Researchers can now rely on this property for every network of that kind, however large or tangled it gets.

Solved without AI

Poincaré conjectureIf every loop in a closed 3D space can shrink to a point, must that space be a 3D sphere?Impact 3/5 · Several research areasHuman proof

What we know

Grigori Perelman published the proof in 2002–03. Mathematicians checked it over several years. Clay awarded the Millennium Prize in 2010.

Why it matters

His work settled how to recognise the three-dimensional sphere and helped classify the possible shapes of three-dimensional spaces.

See the evidence
  • Clay Mathematics Institute · Perelman’s solution and papers
  • Clay Mathematics Institute · Ricci flow and the proof

Still open

P vs NPIf a computer can quickly check an answer, can it quickly find one?Potential impact 5/5 · Everyday systemsOpen

What we know

Nobody has proved whether every problem with a quickly checkable answer can also be solved efficiently. Here, efficient means the work grows at most as a polynomial in the input size.

Why it matters

The 5/5 scenario requires a practical algorithm showing P = NP. It could undermine digital signatures and make complex scheduling far easier. A proof that P ≠ NP would settle a fundamental limit on computing and leave that scenario unrealised.

See the evidence
  • Clay Mathematics Institute · P versus NP
  • Stephen Cook · Official problem description and consequences
Navier–StokesCan the equations for a smoothly moving fluid break down?Potential impact 4/5 · Applied modelsUnder review

What we know

Researchers challenged part of OpenAI’s September proof. A separate November claim for flow without an external force still lacks the public numerical evidence needed for a full check.

Why it matters

Engineers use these equations to model air and water. A verified breakdown would reveal a limit of the equations themselves, even with perfect starting data.

Yang–Mills & the mass gapWhy does it take a minimum amount of energy to excite a quantum field?Potential impact 3/5 · Several research areasPartial result

What we know

An independent group checked OpenAI’s construction of the theory. The second part of the problem remains open. It asks for a proof that the smallest excitation needs more than zero energy.

Why it matters

Physicists use these theories to describe fundamental forces. A proof would explain an energy threshold that experiments and simulations lead them to expect.

Hodge conjectureCan certain hidden features of a shape be built from simpler shapes defined by equations?Potential impact 3/5 · Several research areasOpen

What we know

Mathematicians can do this in special cases. The general conjecture concerns features of shapes defined by polynomial equations.

Why it matters

A proof would let geometers translate between two ways of understanding a shape, by its topology and by its equations.

Birch and Swinnerton-DyerCan one function tell us whether a curve contains infinitely many points with fractional coordinates?Potential impact 3/5 · Several research areasOpen

What we know

The conjecture predicts how many independent rational points an elliptic curve has. Mathematicians have proved important cases. The prediction for all such curves remains open.

Why it matters

A proof would connect a question about fractions to a quantity mathematicians can study using calculus.

The stakes

What if finding a key
became easy?

Explore P vs NP ↗

What we could lose

Payments rely on signatures that are easy to check and hard to forge. If P equals NP and someone finds a practical algorithm, many of those signatures could become easy to forge.

What we could gain

The same algorithm could help a hospital find a rota that covers every shift, or a delivery network find its shortest route. Checking a proposed plan is already the easy part.
